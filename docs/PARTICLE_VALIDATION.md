# Particle validation, v2.5.0

Poor vesicle coverage resulted in a different version of the particle pipeline in AutoMorphoTrack 2.5.0. We skipped MSP04 to MSP06 and MSP10 to MSP12 during parameter selection and used the rest of the 12 movies for development. The 12 movies had been previously assessed with the older fixed configurations, so these split tests assess performance within the reserved movies of the same study, rather than performance in an unseen study. Nine detector settings were evaluated on every fifth development frame. Later, 16 detector and association combinations were assessed on all development frames and 9 synthetic movies with complete point references. Candidate selection required a mean synthetic detection precision of at least 0.90, a mean accepted-link precision of at least 0.95, no more than one retained noise detection per frame and a combined conditional biological accepted-link precision of at least 0.98. Of the settings which met the candidate selection criteria, we selected the settings with the highest mean development accepted-link recall and set the configuration and source hashes before assessing the reserved movies.

The selected detector subtracts a Gaussian background with a 6-pixel standard deviation and applies a scale-normalized Laplacian of Gaussian at 1.5 pixels. Peaks must exceed three times the median-absolute-deviation estimate of response variation and be separated by at least three pixels. Positive foreground is divided into instances using a seeded watershed. Each support mask is constrained to a radius of 4.5 pixels, at least 20% of the peak foreground signal, and at least three pixels. Intensity-weighted centroids of the support masks define positions. These support masks describe locations where the signal is supported. In the current implementation of this algorithm, boundaries of the support masks have not been validated as organelle membranes. Association minimizes squared, distance-normalized assignment cost, with unmatched alternatives and a 6-pixel gate. A link is only accepted if its distance is more than 0.5 pixels better than alternatives, in both assignment directions. Tracks are retained if they contain at least three observations. Tracks with fewer than three observations are excluded from motion summaries and retained masks. Raw detections are not excluded. The selected configuration does not use motion prediction, and gap closing is turned off. Ambiguous links are kept, and are excluded from speed estimates.

Six reserved movies contain 145 annotated trajectories and 2,764 adjacent reference links. Mean accepted-link recall for the frozen default and fixed top-hat configurations was 0.014 and 0.212, respectively. With the particle pipeline, mean accepted-link recall was 0.696, and mean localization recall was 0.768. The new pipeline recovered 2,024 accepted reference links, and four additional accepted links related to different annotated identities, giving conditional precision of 2,024/2,028 among the links with two reference-matched endpoints. Remaining predictions were left unverified. Thirteen trajectories were completely missed, and 33 were fully recovered with accepted links, compared to 41 missed and two fully recovered with the top-hat configuration. Accepted-link recall was 0.739 with a 5-pixel tolerance and 0.587 with a 2-pixel tolerance. Individual reserved movies yielded accepted-link recall from 0.408 to 0.911. Supplementary Fig. 10 and Table 9 show the paired comparisons and their shortcomings.

The new detector together with the previous 6-pixel tracker yielded a mean accepted-link recall of 0.619 on the reserved movies. Replacing the association rule improved recall to 0.696 under the same three-observation requirement. Detection thus mainly drove the improvement, with association adding a smaller gain. Removing the length requirement improved recall to 0.707, but retained a higher number of noise detections. In 30 new synthetic movies spanning two motion patterns, three signal to noise ratios and five random seeds, mean detection precision was 0.967, accepted-link precision was 0.970 and accepted-link recall was 0.803. Five separate noise only movies retained an average of 0.34 detections per frame, as compared to 17.19 prior to the length filter. Analyst-defined controls provide complete references to assess false detections; they do not address biological specificity.

The original 50 synthetic sequences, when rerun, yielded mean Dice scores of 0.877, 0.752 and 0.532, for the new particle masks, the fixed top-hat configuration and the frozen default, respectively. Mean instance precision and accepted-link recall increased from 0.407 to 0.966 and 0.178 to 0.965, respectively. The two baselines reproduced their earlier results to within an absolute error of 10^-12. Transfer to the external mitochondrial masks was limited. Without the length filter, the selected detector yielded mean Dice scores of 0.369 and 0.060 for the seven MoDL SIM patches and the 13 Myofibril patches, respectively. The MoDL score was still below the earlier top-hat result of 0.421. The particle pipeline thus does not replace target-specific segmentation validation for elongated or densely packed mitochondria.

The updated particle pipeline improved recovery of annotated vesicle trajectories. Parameters were set before evaluating reserved movies, which are part of the same, previously examined study. Generalization to other acquisition systems, organelle labels, or biological states remains unresolved. The three-observation requirement reduced noise, and in the case of short-lived events, may have eliminated them. High precision among reference-matched endpoints does not necessarily explain or characterize the many unannotated predictions. The external mitochondrial mask scores were low. Users should review retained and raw detections, set a segmentation target, and verify coverage prior to interpreting counts or motion in a biological context. The baseline set for the original analysis remains, and can be reused to reproduce that work.
